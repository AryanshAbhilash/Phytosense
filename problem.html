The Problem

Farming on a guess, not a reading.

Nearly 86% of India's farms are two hectares or smaller. Their owners make daily decisions about water, fertiliser, and planting with almost no real data — and the cost of a wrong guess is a season's income.

No real-time soil or crop data

Most smallholders judge soil moisture by hand, colour, and habit. Without a way to measure moisture, temperature, or nutrient levels, over-watering and under-fertilising are common — wasting money and reducing yield.

Literacy and language barriers

Agricultural apps and advisories are usually text-heavy and in English or Hindi only. For farmers with limited formal education or who speak a regional dialect, that advice is often unreadable — even when it's correct.

Unpredictable monsoons

Shifting rainfall patterns mean the planting and irrigation calendars farmers grew up with no longer hold. A delayed or erratic monsoon can wipe out a crop planted on traditional timing alone.

Existing tools are built for someone else

Many "smart farming" products assume a smartphone, steady internet, and a large landholding to justify the cost. For a farmer working one or two acres, that price and complexity puts the technology permanently out of reach.
